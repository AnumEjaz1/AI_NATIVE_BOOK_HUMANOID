# Glossary: Digital Twin Simulation Terms

This glossary defines key terms used throughout the Digital Twin Simulation Module (Module 2).

## A

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)**: The simulation of human intelligence processes by machines, especially computer systems. In robotics, AI is used for perception, planning, and control.

## C

**Computer Vision**: A field of artificial intelligence that trains computers to interpret and understand the visual world. In robotics, this involves processing camera and depth sensor data.

**Cross-Platform Development**: Creating applications that can run on multiple operating systems or platforms. Unity supports cross-platform deployment for digital twin applications.

## D

**Digital Twin**: A virtual representation of a physical object or system that spans its lifecycle, is updated with real-time data, and uses simulation, machine learning and reasoning to help decision-making.

**Dynamics**: The branch of mechanics concerned with the motion of bodies under the action of forces. In robotics, this refers to how forces affect robot movement.

## G

**Gazebo**: A 3D simulation environment for robotics that provides accurate physics simulation, realistic rendering, and support for robot control programs.

**Gaussian Noise**: A type of statistical noise having a probability density function equal to that of the normal distribution. Commonly used to model sensor noise.

## I

**Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)**: An electronic device that measures and reports a body's specific force, angular rate, and sometimes the magnetic field surrounding the body.

**Integration**: The process of combining different systems or components to work together as a unified whole. In this context, it refers to connecting Gazebo, Unity, and ROS 2.

## L

**LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging)**: A remote sensing method that uses light in the form of a pulsed laser to measure distances and create 3D maps of the environment.

## P

**Physically Based Rendering (PBR)**: A method of shading and rendering that more accurately calculates light-object interactions to produce photorealistic results.

**Physics Engine**: Software that simulates the behavior of physical objects using mathematical models to represent physical phenomena like gravity, collisions, and forces.

## R

**Real-time Rendering**: The process of generating computer graphics instantaneously to produce interactive visual output at a high frame rate, typically 30-60 frames per second.

**Robot Operating System 2 (ROS 2)**: Flexible framework for writing robot software. It's a collection of tools, libraries, and conventions that aim to simplify the task of creating complex and robust robot behavior across a wide variety of robot platforms.

**ROS Bridge**: A connection that allows communication between ROS-based systems and other frameworks (e.g., Unity, web applications).

## S

**Sensor Fusion**: The process of combining data from multiple sensors to produce more accurate and reliable information than could be obtained from any single sensor.

**Simulation**: The imitation of the operation of a real-world process or system over time. In robotics, this involves modeling physical systems and their behaviors.

**Synchronization**: The coordination of events to operate in unison. In digital twins, this refers to keeping simulation states consistent across different systems.

## U

**Unity**: A cross-platform game engine used for creating video games, interactive 3D applications, and digital twins with high-fidelity visual rendering.

## V

**Virtual Reality (VR)**: A simulated experience that can be similar to or completely different from the real world, often used for immersive digital twin visualization.

## W

**World File**: In Gazebo, an SDF (Simulation Description Format) file that defines the environment for a simulation, including physics parameters, lighting, and objects.

## Z

**Zero Mean Noise**: A type of noise where the average value of the noise signal is zero, commonly used in sensor modeling.
